# Craft Notes

Short, attributed reminders to keep beside the templates. These are sparks — not a substitute for the full courses or craft books they come from.

## Alan Moore — on being a writer

> Accept any work you're offered that's legal and morally tolerable, try to do as good a job as you can and learn from the resultant mess.

> Stand tall with your pen. You should never think of yourself as purely an entertainer for hire who is lucky to have the work… if you are a writer, then you are having an effect upon human history.

> Everybody can write. Perhaps, everybody should write.

> Will is the single most important element because, without it, you will not finish the shortest of stories… If you don't finish your work and give it to somebody to appraise, then it will never get published.

> Always remember that the plot is not the story. It's just what gets you from one end of the story to the other.

> Feel compassion for your most villainous character… For your heroes to have any weight and resonance, you had better ensure your villains have the same.

> Even if it throws your plot off, you should always listen to what your characters are saying.

> If we talk about something specific with enough passion, detail and reality, then it becomes universal… If we see an experience we thought was uniquely ours in a painting, film or novel, we feel less alone.

*Source: Alan Moore, Storytelling (BBC Maestro course notes).*

## Structure reminders (Save the Cat–style)

- Opening image ↔ Final image should rhyme.
- Theme is stated early; the finale proves it through action.
- Fun and Games delivers the promise of the premise.
- Midpoint raises stakes and often flips the emotional trajectory.
- All Is Lost needs a whiff of death (literal or metaphorical).
- Finale works well as a five-point sequence: plan → execute → surprise failure → dig deep → new plan.

## How to use this note

Link it from a project dashboard when you need a pep talk, or pull one quote into a scene card when you stall.
